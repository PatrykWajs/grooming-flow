# Style rules in plain words (for the owner's review)

Source: Polina's seminar deck "Grooming - Fashion Consulting & Coaching" (built on Real Men Real Style and Gentleman's Gazette), transcribed verbatim on 2026-07-12 (`s001`-`s106`) and re-captured as 107 screenshots on 2026-11-03 (all matched, no new slides). Room photos of the second session are cited as `room-NNN`. Every rule below cites its slide. Where the deck is silent the engine scores neutral (0.5); nothing here is invented.

Scores: a pair score near 1 means "the deck recommends this together", near 0 "the deck advises against it"; 0.5 is neutral. Hard rules reject an outfit outright. The weight of the whole style block in the final ranking is 40 %.

Colour words map to the app's 24-colour palette: "blue trousers" = navy / blue / denim, "light blue" = light-blue, "light pink" = pink, "light grey" = light-grey, "camel" = tan, "maroon-brown" = burgundy.

## A. Belt and shoes

- **R01 (hard, match)** The belt strap matches the shoes: black shoes take a black belt, brown-family shoes (brown, tan, dark brown, burgundy) take a brown-family belt. `s003`
- **R02 (hard, never)** Black shoes with a brown belt, or brown shoes with a black belt, never. `s003`
- **R03 (hard, formality)** Belt and shoes stay within one formality step of each other (thin dress belt with dress shoes, wide casual belt with casual shoes). `s003`
- **R04 (soft, palette)** Basic belt colours are black and brown; another belt colour scores 0.4. `s003`
- **R01b (hard, never)** Fabric belts (woven, canvas) never with dress shoes (derbies, oxfords, loafers): the shoe-by-belt matrix pairs them only with sneakers, chukkas and casual boots. `room-026`
- **R01c (exemption)** White sneakers take any belt, fabric belts (woven, canvas) cross the leather families, and blue dress shoes take a black, dark-brown or blue belt; all are exempt from R01 and R02. `room-026`
- The matrix as read from the photo (`room-026`): black oxfords → black leather; blue formal → black, dark brown, blue leather; brown derby → light brown; cognac loafer → light brown, cognac; dark brown casual → dark brown, cognac, grey canvas, print fabric; multi-colour sneaker → grey canvas, print fabric; oxblood formal → dark brown; tan chukka boots → light brown, grey canvas, print fabric; white sneaker → every belt.
- Not representable, kept for the owner: unusual buckles only with jeans (`s003`), patent leather not with jeans (`s006`), buckle on the fly line, third hole (`s003`, `s004`).

## B. Trousers and tops: colour pairing

- **R05 (palette)** Base trouser colours: black, blue (navy, blue, denim), grey (grey, charcoal), beige. `s049`, `s071`
- **R06 (palette)** Base shirt and top colours: white, soft pink, light blue, light grey, blue. `s050`, `s071`
- **R07 (pair 0.9)** Blue trousers with a white, light-blue, pink or light-grey top. `s051`
- **R08 (pair 0.9)** Grey trousers with a white, pink, light-blue or blue top. `s052`
- **R09 (pair 0.85)** Black trousers with light tops (white, cream, light-blue, pink, light-grey, beige) so the look does not get too dark. `s053`
- **R10 (pair 0.7)** Black trousers with black tops: total black works. `s053`
- **R11 (pair 0.8)** Black trousers with a grey or camel jacket to break the monochrome. `s053`
- **R12 (pair 0.9)** Beige trousers with white. `s054`
- **R13 (pair 0.85)** Beige trousers with dark green, navy or blue tops. `s054`
- **R14 (pair 0.65)** Beige trousers with brown or black tops: "maybe, depends on the shades". `s054`
- **R15 (pair 0.9)** Blue, grey or beige trousers with brown or black shoes, or white sneakers. `s051`, `s052`, `s054`
- **R16 (pair 0.85)** A white top with any trouser colour: the safest choice. `s077`
- **R17 (pair 0.85)** Grey with white; grey is the wildcard. `s077`
- **R18 (pair 0.75)** A grey item next to a bright item: grey calms a bold outfit. `s077`
- **R19 (pair 0.8)** Black next to light colours: black gives intensity to a light outfit. `s077`
- **R20 (lightness 0.8)** Light on top, dark on the bottom; the reverse scores 0.45 (the deck says "generally", so dark tops are discouraged, not banned). `s059`
- **R21 (soft, avoid 0.3)** Shorts are avoided. `s059` (the occasion rules of the app already ban them at the office)

## C. Patterns

- **R22 (pair)** Solid with solid 0.7, solid with a pattern 0.7, pattern with pattern 0.35: solids are the safest and go with everything. `s065`
- **R23 (hard, limit)** At most two patterned items in an outfit (the deck allows two or three patterns "with care"; the engine stops at two). `room-014`
- **R24 (formality adjust)** Stripes keep the item's formality; checks are more informal (-0.5); prints are the most informal (-1). `s066`, `s067`, `s068`
- **R25 (pair 0.85)** A checked shirt under a sweater, or unbuttoned over a t-shirt. `s067`
- **R26 (formality adjust)** A fine check is dressier than a bold check: the user marks a bold check as `print` and it takes R24's -1. `s082`
- Not representable: stripe width and multi-colour stripes (`s066`), bold versus subtle print (`s068`); the user can lower an item's formality by hand.

## D. Formality and the occasion

- **R27 (hard, formality)** The outfit's items stay within two formality steps of each other; within one step scores 1.0, two steps 0.6. Neither overdressed nor underdressed. `s064`, `s091`, `s058`
- **R28 (pair 0.7)** A blazer over a t-shirt or pullover reads casual and is allowed. `s060`
- **R29 (pair 0.9)** A blazer over a dress shirt. `s060`
- **R30 (hard, occasion)** Tracksuits and joggers only for gym or home. `s086`
- **R31 (pair)** Athletic (running) shoes with joggers 0.8, with jeans 0.7, with chinos 0.4, with dress trousers 0.2: wearable outside the gym but the formality drops. `s098`
- **R32 (soft, avoid 0.3)** Sandals and flip-flops: strictly the beach. `s099`
- **R33 (formality adjust)** Jeans: the darker the more formal. Black or navy jeans +0.5; light-blue jeans -0.5. `s085`
- **R34 (scale)** Trouser formality from casual to formal: denim < drawstring wool < chinos < cotton dress trousers < wool dress trousers; the taxonomy defaults follow it (jeans 2, chinos 3, dress trousers 4, suit trousers 5). `room-015`
- **R35 (scale)** Dress-code spectrum, formal to casual: white tie, black tie, black tie creative, black tie optional, business formal, cocktail, semi-formal, business casual, smart casual, casual, ultra casual. Mapped to the app's 1-5: 5 = black/white tie and business formal, 4 = cocktail and semi-formal, 3 = business casual and smart casual, 2 = casual, 1 = ultra casual and athletic. `room-028`

## E. Layering and outerwear

- **R36 (hard, limit)** No more than three visible layers (base, layer, outer). `s060`, `room-025`
- **R37 (lightness 0.8)** Darker colours on the outside: the outer layer darker than the base; the reverse scores 0.4. `room-025`
- **R38 (pair 0.8)** Overcoat over a suit jacket or blazer: formal coats belong over tailoring. `s005`
- **R39 (pair 0.8)** Trench coat with jeans: formal enough for jeans. `s014`
- **R40 (pair 0.4)** Trench coat with a suit jacket: too casual for a suit (the deck allows it only to dress a suit down). `s014`
- **R41 (palette)** Classic coat colours: grey, beige, camel, blue, navy, black; polo coats beige or camel. Other coat colours score 0.5. `s025`, `s007`
- Not representable: coat length, sleeve length, belted coats for tall men (`s017`, `s018`), chesterfield details (`s025`).

## F. Shoes

- **R42 (palette)** Dress shoes come in black and brown (tan, burgundy); dress shoes in another colour score 0.4. `s100`
- **R43 (palette)** Casual sneakers come in white, black, light grey or blue; other sneaker colours score 0.4. `s101`
- Not representable: never a square toe (`s104`), avoid rhinestone or star-logo and chunky trainers (`s102`), youthful evening shoes (`s105`), Oxford and monk-strap formality scales (`room-017`, `room-021`), fitting rules (`s103`).

## G. Accessories

- **R44 (hard, requires)** A necklace is worn only with a t-shirt. `s046`
- **R45 (hard, never)** The pocket square never matches the tie exactly (same colour and same pattern). `s028`
- **R46 (formality adjust)** A white or muted pocket square is formal (+1); a bold colour or pattern is casual (-1 when patterned). `s028`
- **R47 (palette)** Tie colours: dark (navy, purple, dark green, burgundy) or light (light blue, pink, orange); a subtle pattern is nicer than fully plain. `s020`
- **R48 (formality by type)** Dress watch 4 (fits formal wear), sport or field or chronograph watch 2, smartwatch 1 (gym only by formality). Enforced through R27. `s034`, `s036`, `s038`, `s040`
- **R49 (hard, never)** A sport watch or smartwatch with a suit jacket or tie. `s036`, `s040`
- Not representable: tie length and width (`s023`, `s026`), tie clip size and position (`s048`), cufflinks (`s031`-`s033`), ring meanings (`room-040`), sunglasses by face shape (`room-039`), socks (not tracked, `s029`, `room-027`).
